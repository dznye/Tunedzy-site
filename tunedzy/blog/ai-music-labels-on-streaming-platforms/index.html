AI Music Labels on Streaming Platforms: What Artists Should Check

Updated October 2026 · AI music and song tools

Quick answer

Several streaming platforms have been adding policies on AI-generated music, including requests for disclosure and rules on impersonation. Before you upload, read the current policy of each platform, check whether it asks for a label, confirm your rights to every element, and keep records of how the music was made.

Streaming platforms distribute millions of tracks, and they have had to decide how to handle music made with AI. Some have added policies that ask for disclosure of AI use, restrict the impersonation of real artists, or remove tracks that break their rules. For artists, these policies change what a responsible upload looks like. This post explains why the policies are appearing, what artists should check and how to disclose AI use in a way that protects the release.

The policies differ between platforms, and they change over time. This post describes the general direction and the questions to ask. It does not summarise any single platform’s rules, which you should read directly.

Why platforms are adding policies

Platforms face several pressures at once. Listeners want to know what they are hearing. Artists want fair treatment and protection from impersonation. Rights holders want their catalogues protected. And the sheer volume of uploads makes manual checking hard, so platforms are adding rules and automated checks. Policies on AI music are one result of these pressures.

Expect the rules to keep changing as the tools improve and as legislation develops in different countries.

Common types of rule

Some platforms ask uploaders to say whether AI tools were used in making a track. Some restrict the use of a real artist’s voice or likeness without permission. Some remove tracks that copy existing recordings or compositions. Some apply labels to generated music in the catalogue. The details differ, so read each platform’s current guidance before uploading.

Some rules apply to the distributor you use as well as the platform. Your distributor may have its own requirements, and it may ask for information you need to provide.

What to check before upload

  1. Read the current AI and impersonation policy of each platform you plan to use.
  2. Check whether the platform or your distributor asks for a disclosure, and how it should be made.
  3. Confirm the rights to every element, including samples, stems, lyrics and any voice.
  4. Check the terms of any tool used to make the music, including what it says about ownership.
  5. Keep a record of how the track was made, with dates and the tools used.

How to disclose honestly

If a platform asks about AI use, answer accurately and in full. A short, honest description is better than a vague claim. Explain which parts were generated, which were written or performed by you, and which tools were used. If you are unsure whether a use needs disclosure, disclose it. Platforms can act on a false claim, and an honest disclosure is much easier to defend than a missing one.

Keep the wording of your disclosure consistent across releases, so listeners and platforms can see your practice. Consistency also makes it easier to answer a distributor or a platform later. Keep a copy of each disclosure with the release file.

Impersonation and voice

A track that imitates a real artist’s voice can be removed, even if it is labelled as a fan project or a tribute. Platforms are increasingly strict about impersonation, and the policy may apply regardless of whether the track is commercial. Use your own voice, a licensed performer or a clearly fictional voice, and do not imitate a real artist without permission.

What to do if a track is removed

If a track is removed, read the reason in the notice, check your records, and respond through the platform’s appeal process if you believe the removal was wrong. Keep copies of everything. If the removal affects your income, take advice from a professional who knows the platform’s rules and the law where you live.

Plan the upload around the policy

Treat the policy check as part of the release schedule, not an afterthought. Allow time before a planned release date to read the rules, answer any disclosure questions and fix problems if a track is flagged. A release that is rushed past its disclosure form is more likely to be held or removed, which can damage the launch more than a short delay would.

When you work with a distributor, ask for its requirements in writing and keep the reply. If a platform later questions a release, that record shows what you were told and when.

Limits

Platform policies change, and this post cannot cover every rule. The general principles are stable: check the policy, confirm your rights, disclose honestly and keep records. For a significant release, read the current policies directly and take advice where needed.

Keep the release clean

A clean, well-mixed release is less likely to raise questions and more likely to be enjoyed. The LUFS meter can check the loudness and true peak of your final file before upload. Frequently asked questions

Do platforms require disclosure of AI music?

Some do, in different ways. Check each platform’s current policy before uploading.

Can I release a track that sounds like a famous artist?

Not without permission. Impersonation policies can remove a track whatever it is called.

What if I forget to disclose?

Correct it as soon as you notice. Platforms can act on missing or false information.

Does my distributor have rules too?

Often yes. Check the requirements of your distributor as well as the platforms.
