How to Choose the Best Take for a Voiceover

Updated October 2026 · Voiceover and narration

Quick answer

Listen to each take twice: once for meaning and feeling, and once for clean sound. Compare the top two or three takes against each other, not against your memory of the script, and stop when another take is not clearly better.

Recording a voiceover often produces five or six takes of each line. Picking the right one is a skill, and it is easy to get wrong when you are tired. The temptation is to choose the most polished take, or the last one, when the best take is the one that sounds most like someone talking to a listener.

Listen for meaning first

On the first listen, ignore the sound quality and ask whether the take says what the line means. Does the stress fall on the right word? Does it sound like a real thought? A take with a small flaw but a clear meaning is usually better than a flawless take with no life in it.

Listen again for clean sound

On the second listen, check the sound. Look for clicks, mouth noise, breath pops, room noise and any change in distance from the microphone. Use headphones, and turn the volume up slightly, since problems that hide at normal level often show up quietly at a higher one.

Compare two or three, not all of them

Narrow the takes to the best two or three, then compare them one after another, with a few seconds between. Comparing many takes at once is tiring and leads to indecision. Mark your favourite, then listen to it again in the context of the lines before and after it.

Keep the sound consistent

When you have a favourite, check that it matches the other lines in level, distance and tone. A take that is clearer but noticeably quieter can stand out in the edit. The loudness guide explains the level targets that keep a series of lines consistent.

Know when to stop

If the next take is not clearly better, stop and use the current one. Perfectionism wastes time and drains the voice, and the listener will not hear the difference between the fifth and the sixth take. Keep a note of the take number, so you can find it again.

Limits

Your ear gets tired during long sessions, so choose takes in short bursts. If you are unsure, sleep on it and listen again the next morning. A fresh ear hears the difference more clearly.

Frequently asked questions

How many takes is enough?

Usually three to five per line. Stop when the next take is not clearly better.

Should I choose the most polished take?

Not always. Meaning and natural delivery matter more than a flawless but lifeless read.

What if two takes are equal?

Choose the one that matches the lines around it in level and tone.

Should I listen on speakers or headphones?

Headphones show the noise and mouth sounds most clearly. Check on a speaker too, if you can.
