How to Set Up a Microphone Stand for Voice

Updated October 2026 · Mics and recording gear

Quick answer

Set the stand on a stable base, raise the microphone to mouth height, and angle it slightly off-axis from the mouth. Keep the stand away from the desk and the chair, tighten all the joints, and test for vibration before you record.

A microphone stand looks simple, but a wobbly or badly placed stand causes many recording problems. It moves when you move, it transmits vibration, and it can change the distance without you noticing. A stable setup is more important than an expensive microphone.

Choose a stable base

A heavy, wide base keeps the stand still. A thin or light base tips easily and sends vibration into the microphone. If the stand is on a desk, check that the desk does not rattle when you tap it.

Set the height and angle

Raise the microphone to mouth height, or slightly below, so you can speak without leaning. Angle the capsule slightly off-axis from the mouth, so breath does not hit it directly. Mark the position on the stand or the desk, so you can return to it.

Keep it away from noise

Place the stand away from the chair, the keyboard and the desk edge. Avoid putting it on a surface that carries footsteps or fan vibration. A small pad under the base can help.

Tighten and test

Tighten all the joints, then record a short test while you tap the desk gently. Listen on headphones for thumps, rumble and changes in the voice. Adjust the stand, then record again until it is quiet.

Limits

A stand cannot fix a noisy room, and a heavy stand may be harder to move around. Choose a stand that suits your space, and keep the setup stable from session to session.

Frequently asked questions

What height should a mic stand be?

Around mouth height, or slightly below, so you can speak without leaning.

Why does my voice change when I move?

The distance or angle changes. Mark the position and keep your seat the same.

Does a heavier stand help?

Usually yes, because it is more stable and carries less vibration.

Should the stand touch the desk?

It can, but a soft pad under the base reduces vibration.
