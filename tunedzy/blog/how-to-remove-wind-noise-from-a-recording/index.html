How to Remove Wind Noise From a Recording

Updated October 2026 · Audio repair and clean-up

Quick answer

Wind noise is a rumbling, low-frequency sound caused by moving air across the microphone. The best fix is prevention: use a windscreen or a furry cover and move the microphone out of the wind. A filter can reduce the rumble, but it cannot restore speech that the wind has covered.

Wind noise sounds like a roar or a rumble, and it can bury speech on an outdoor recording. It is caused by air moving over the microphone capsule, not by the sound you want to capture. That is why it is so hard to remove afterwards: the wind is not the same as the voice, and it often covers the same frequencies.

Prevent it before you record

Use a foam windscreen, and for strong wind a furry cover, which breaks up the air movement. Shelter the microphone behind a person, a wall or a car, and keep it out of the direct path of the breeze. Record when the wind is lower, or move to a more sheltered spot. Prevention beats any cure.

Check the placement

A microphone pointing into the wind picks up much more noise than one pointing across it. Turn the microphone slightly, so the wind passes over the front rather than straight into the capsule. Keep the same distance from the speaker, so the voice sounds consistent.

Use a high-pass filter carefully

A high-pass filter removes low frequencies, which reduces the rumble. Set the cutoff low enough that the voice keeps its body. If you cut too high, the speech becomes thin and tinny. Listen carefully and adjust in small steps, rather than cutting the low end hard.

Reduce what remains

For steady wind noise, a gentle noise reducer can help, but it may leave a watery sound. Use it lightly, and compare with the original. The Noise-Free Voice Recorder can reduce background noise on speech, though strong wind is still best prevented.

Limits

When wind has covered the words, no filter can bring them back. In that case, re-record the line, or use a different take. A short, clean take in a sheltered spot is worth more than a long, repaired one.

Frequently asked questions

Is a windscreen enough?

For light breeze, usually yes. Strong wind needs a furry cover or a sheltered spot.

Can I remove wind noise completely in editing?

Rarely. Filters reduce the rumble but cannot restore speech that the wind has covered.

Should I cut all the low end to remove wind?

No. It thins the voice. Use a high-pass filter gently and listen carefully.

Does a phone need a windscreen?

Often, yes. A phone microphone is small and sensitive to wind, so a cover helps.
