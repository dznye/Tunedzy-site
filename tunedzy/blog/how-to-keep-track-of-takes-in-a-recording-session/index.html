How to Keep Track of Takes in a Recording Session

Updated October 2026 · Recording workflow

Quick answer

Keep a simple take log with the take number, the time, a line of the script and a short note about how it went. Mark your favourite takes as you record, and name each file with its take number, so you can find the best version quickly during editing.

A session with many takes can turn into a pile of files, and the best take is lost. Without a record, editing becomes a search through every file. A take log takes a few seconds per take, and it makes the edit much faster and less stressful.

Keep a simple take log

Write the take number, the time, the line or section, and a short note. A note such as “good energy, breath at end” is enough to decide later. Use a notebook or a text file next to the recording, and keep it simple.

Mark the good takes

When you hear a take that works, mark it in the log, and in the file name if you can. A marker in the file or the recorder is also useful. Do not wait until the end of the session, because the good moments are easy to forget.

Name each take clearly

Include the take number and the section in the file name, such as intro_take04. Consistent names let you find any take by a quick search. The file naming post covers the full pattern.

Keep the performance notes

Note the problems too, such as a loud breath, a stumble or a noise outside. These notes help you decide which take to use, and they guide the next take. Write them as you go, while the detail is fresh.

Use the log in the edit

When you edit, start with the marked takes. Compare the best two or three against each other, rather than scanning every file. The log turns a long search into a short comparison.

Limits

A take log adds a small step to each session, and it is easy to skip when things are going well. Keep it simple enough to use every time, and it will pay off in the edit.

Frequently asked questions

How detailed should the log be?

Short. A take number, a time, the section and a one-line note is enough.

Should I mark takes on the recorder?

If your recorder allows it, yes. A marker helps, but the log is the main record.

What if I forget to log a take?

Listen to the takes in order during the edit, and note the good ones then.

How many takes should I keep?

Keep all of them until the edit is finished, then archive the ones you did not use.
