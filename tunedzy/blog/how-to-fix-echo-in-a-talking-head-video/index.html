How to Fix Echo in a Talking-Head Video

Updated October 2026 · Video sound

Quick answer

Echo in a talking-head video comes from the room. Move the microphone closer, add soft surfaces behind and beside you, and record in a space with rugs and fabrics. Cleanup can reduce a little echo afterwards, but it cannot make a bare room sound close.

A talking-head video with echo sounds like the speaker is standing in an empty hall. It is one of the most common problems in home video, and it makes viewers trust the sound less than the picture. The fix starts with the room, because no filter can fully remove the reflections that a hard room creates.

Choose a softer room

Pick the room with the most soft surfaces: carpet, curtains, sofas, cushions and bookshelves full of books. Bare walls, glass and tiled floors reflect sound. Test the room by clapping once and listening for a ringing tail. The clap test guide shows what to listen for.

Place the microphone close

Put the microphone as close to the speaker as the frame allows, usually a few tens of centimetres from the mouth, pointing toward the mouth and slightly off-axis. A closer microphone picks up more of the voice and less of the room. A clip-on microphone on the chest is often the simplest way to get a close, dry sound.

Soften the space behind the speaker

The sound that comes back from the wall behind the speaker is the main source of echo in many videos. Hang a blanket, put a cushion or a rug in the background, or move the chair away from the wall. A soft backdrop helps both the sound and the picture.

Clean up what is left

After recording, reduce background noise gently, and avoid heavy processing that makes the voice watery. The Vocal Checker shows clipping, noise, rumble and level problems in a recording, which helps you decide what to fix.

Check on a laptop and a phone

Play the finished video on a laptop speaker and on a phone, since many viewers use them. If the voice still sounds distant, record again in a softer space, rather than adding more processing.

Limits

A very bright room or a large empty space will always sound echoey. Cleanup helps a little. The most useful change is usually a closer microphone and a few soft objects around the speaker.

Frequently asked questions

Can I remove echo completely in editing?

Rarely. Editing can reduce some echo, but the best result comes from a softer room and a closer microphone.

Does a better camera fix echo?

No. Echo is a room and microphone problem, not a camera problem.

Where should I put the microphone?

As close to the speaker as the frame allows, pointing at the mouth, and slightly off-axis to reduce breath.

Is a blanket behind me enough?

Often it helps a lot. Add a rug or cushions if the room is still bright.
