How Much Headroom to Leave in Audio and Why

Updated October 2026 · Loudness and levels

Quick answer

Leave a few decibels of headroom below full scale for speech, more if the mix is loud or heavily processed. Headroom protects the sound during conversion and playback, so keep the peaks below full scale and check the true peak of the exported file.

Headroom is the space between the loudest moment of a recording and the point where digital audio clips. A mix with plenty of headroom can be converted, processed and played back without distortion. A mix with none sounds harsh the moment anything adds gain, which happens often.

What headroom protects against

Headroom gives room for the things that happen after you finish mixing. Encoding to MP3 or AAC can add small overshoots. Playback devices and streaming services may adjust the level. A listener may turn the volume up. Each of these can push a loud peak over full scale, and headroom absorbs that.

How much to leave

For speech, a few decibels of headroom below full scale is a common and sensible choice. Music that is heavily compressed or limited may need more, or the margin disappears on export. There is no single correct number, because it depends on the content and the format. Choose a margin you can explain, then check it on the exported file.

Where headroom disappears

  • Stacked gain from many effects, each adding a little level.
  • Heavy limiting pushed to make the file louder, which removes the margin.
  • Boosting after processing to match a target, without rechecking the peaks.
  • Normalising to the peak, which sets the loudest sample to full scale and leaves nothing spare.

Check headroom on the export

Check the true peak of the final file, not only the project. The LUFS meter shows the peak alongside the loudness, so you can see both at once. If the peak is too close to full scale, lower the level before export, rather than fixing it later.

Limits

Headroom is a safety margin, not a sound. A file with a generous margin can still sound quiet or thin, so aim for the right balance between level and headroom. The goal is a clean, steady sound with no clipping, not the lowest possible peak.

Frequently asked questions

Is zero headroom ever acceptable?

It is risky. A file at full scale can clip after conversion or playback adjustment.

Does headroom make audio quieter?

It leaves a margin below full scale. Your loudness target still sets the overall level.

How do I check headroom after export?

Measure the true peak of the exported file, not the project, and compare it with your margin.

Is headroom the same as dynamic range?

No. Headroom is the space to full scale. Dynamic range is the difference between the loud and quiet parts.
