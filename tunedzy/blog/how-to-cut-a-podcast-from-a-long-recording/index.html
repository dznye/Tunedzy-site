How to Cut a Podcast Episode From a Long Recording

Updated October 2026 · Podcast sound and editing

Quick answer

Cut a long recording in three passes: mark the best moments, cut the repeats and tangents, then tidy the joins. Work from the transcript where you can, and keep the original recording untouched.

An hour of conversation can become a twenty-minute episode, but only if you cut with a plan. Random cutting leaves awkward joins and loses the thread. A three-pass method keeps the story intact, and it gets faster the more episodes you do.

Before you start: save the original

Copy the full recording to a separate folder and do not touch it again. Work on a project that points to the copy. If you later want a longer version, a clip for social media or a re-cut after feedback, the original is still there. This one habit saves you from re-recording.

Pass one: mark the moments that matter

Listen once at normal speed and mark the parts you would keep if you had to choose three minutes. Look for a clear point, a good story, an answer that lands, a laugh that feels real. Ignore the rest for now. You are looking for the spine of the episode, which is usually shorter than you expect.

Pass two: cut by the words

Open the transcript and cut the repeats, tangents, false starts and dead air. Editing by text is faster than the waveform, and you can read the flow of the conversation as it will sound. The edit by words guide explains how to do this in the Tunedzy transcript editor, including how to check each cut by ear.

Pass three: tidy the joins

Every cut is a join, and a join is where listeners notice the edit. Play each one at normal volume and at full speed. If the room tone jumps, add a short crossfade or a little room tone under the join. Remove a breath or a click only where it is distracting, because a natural breath keeps the voice human. The crossfade guide has the steps.

Keep the natural pauses

Pauses are part of how people think and talk. Remove the long dead air that drags, but keep short pauses before an important point or after a joke. A conversation with every gap removed sounds rushed and robotic.

Check the finished episode

Play the episode from start to end, on headphones and on a phone speaker. Check that the story still makes sense without the cut material, that the speakers sound like themselves, and that nothing repeats. Note the length of the final episode, then compare it with your plan.

Limits

Heavy cutting can change the meaning of an answer, so keep the context around every quote. If you are editing someone else’s words, agree the approach first. Good editing is mostly removing what does not help the listener, not making the guest sound better than they were.

Frequently asked questions

How long does cutting take?

It depends on the length and the material. A one-hour recording can take a few hours the first time and less as you get faster.

Should I cut before or after cleaning?

Clean first, then cut. Cutting first can leave the noise changing at each join.

Can I cut by the transcript?

Yes, with a transcript editor such as the one in Tunedzy. Always check each cut by ear before you publish.

Will listeners notice the cuts?

Only if the joins are rough. Matched room tone and natural pauses make most cuts hard to hear.
