How to Get a Weeknd-Style Vocal Sound at Home

Updated October 2026 · Vocal sound by style

Quick answer

The sound people mean is a bright, close, airy lead vocal, thickened with doubles, with a long reverb and a delay behind it. You can get the same kind of sound with a clean recording, light tuning, a high-shelf lift, doubled choruses and a reverb and delay sent in parallel. It will sound like that style, not like the person.

Step 1: Start with a clean, close take

Record close to the mic, with a pop filter, in a quiet room. A dry, clean take gives every later step something to work with. Remove noise and room echo before adding effects.

Step 2: Brighten and open the top

A gentle high-shelf boost above roughly 8 to 10 kHz adds air. Do it after de-essing, or the S sounds will jump out.

Step 3: Double the chorus

Record the chorus twice more and pan them a little left and right, quieter than the lead. The doubles give width without making the lead louder.

Step 4: Add space on sends

  1. Send the vocal to a long, dark reverb and keep the return low, so it sits behind the voice.
  2. Send it to a delay set to a rhythmic value, such as a quarter or eighth note, and filter the low end of the echoes.
  3. Duck the reverb and delay slightly while the singer is singing, so words stay clear.

Limits

This describes the general style, not any artist’s actual settings, which we do not know. A voice, a performance and a mix cannot be copied by a chain. Tunedzy is not affiliated with any artist.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need pitch correction for this sound?

Light, transparent tuning helps keep the lead steady, but the style is mostly tone, doubles and space.

Which reverb should I use?

A long, dark reverb on a send, mixed low. Try a plate or hall and shorten it if the words get blurry.

Can a tool copy an artist exactly?

No. You can get close to a style, not to a person.
