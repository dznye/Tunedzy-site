How to Set Up a Boom Arm for a Microphone

Updated October 2026 · Mics and recording gear

Quick answer

Set the boom arm so the microphone sits a hand’s width or two from the mouth, slightly off-axis and at mouth height. Balance the arm so it stays where you put it, route the cable so it does not pull, and isolate the arm from the desk to stop vibration.

A boom arm holds the microphone in the same place every session, and it frees the desk. Once it is set up well, the microphone stays close and steady, which is the most important part of clear speech. A poorly set boom arm can sag, vibrate or pick up cable noise, so the setup is worth doing carefully.

Position the microphone

Bring the arm over the desk so the microphone sits a hand’s width or two from the mouth, slightly to one side. Keep it at mouth height, or a little below, so you do not have to lean. Mark the position on the desk, so you can return to it.

Balance the arm

Most arms have a tension adjustment. Set it so the microphone stays where you leave it, without drooping, and without needing a strong grip to move it. A well-balanced arm lets you adjust with one hand.

Route the cable

Run the cable along the arm and secure it with clips or ties, leaving slack at each joint. A tight cable pulls on the microphone and transmits noise. Keep the cable away from power cables, to reduce hum.

Stop vibration

Desks transmit vibration, from keyboard taps, footsteps and the chair. Clamp the arm to a sturdy part of the desk or a stand, and place a soft pad under the clamp if you can. Test by tapping the desk while recording a short sample.

Check the sound

Record a test, listen on headphones, and check for pops, rumble and cable noise. Adjust the position and the arm, then record again. The clap test helps you find the best spot in the room.

Limits

A boom arm does not fix a noisy room or a poor microphone. It makes the setup consistent, which helps every recording sound more even. Treat it as a tool for stability, not a cure for everything else.

Frequently asked questions

How close should the microphone be?

About a hand’s width or two from the mouth, slightly off-axis.

Why does my boom arm drift down?

The tension is too loose. Adjust it so the arm holds its position.

How do I stop desk noise?

Clamp the arm to a sturdy part of the desk, add a soft pad, and avoid tapping the desk while recording.

Should the cable be tight?

No. Leave slack at each joint, so the cable does not pull on the microphone.
