How Loud Should Vocals Be on a Beat?

Updated October 2026 · Vocal recording fixes

Quick answer

There is no fixed number. Set the vocal so the words are clear over the loudest part of the beat, then check the balance on phone speakers, earphones and a car or a laptop. Many producers keep vocals a few decibels above the beat in the chorus, but the right level is the one that carries the lyric.

A method that works on any beat

  1. Play the loudest section of the beat, then bring the vocal up until the words sit clearly on top without feeling shouted.
  2. Check the lyric. If you have to strain to hear a word, the vocal is too low or buried in the low mids.
  3. Check the peaks: the loudest words should not clip. Use the LUFS meter to see the overall level of the finished mix.

Test it where people will listen

Most listeners use phone speakers or earphones. A vocal that sounds right on monitors can disappear on a phone, so play the mix on at least two other devices and adjust.

Limits

Genre, tempo and the singer change the balance. A fast rap verse and a slow ballad need different levels. Use the method, not a number from a chart.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a standard dB level for vocals?

No. The balance depends on the song. Set it by the lyric, then check it on several speakers.

Should the vocal be louder than the beat?

Usually the vocal sits on top of the beat for clarity, but the exact difference depends on the mix.

What is the first thing to check?

Whether you can hear every word over the loudest part of the beat, on a phone speaker.
