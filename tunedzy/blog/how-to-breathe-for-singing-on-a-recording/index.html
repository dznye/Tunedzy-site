How to Breathe for Singing on a Recording

Updated October 2026 · Singing at home

Quick answer

Plan your breaths before you record, marking them on the lyric sheet at natural phrase ends. Breathe low and relaxed, with the shoulders loose, and take the breath before the phrase begins. Keep breaths quiet, and leave the natural ones in the take, since they help the performance feel alive.

Breath in a recording is easy to hear. A gasp before a phrase or a loud intake can spoil a good take. Good breathing for a recording is quiet, planned and relaxed. Most of the work is done before you press record, not during the take.

Plan the breaths

Read through the lyrics and mark where you will breathe. Choose points at the ends of phrases, where the music rests, and avoid breathing in the middle of a word. Write the marks on the sheet, so you do not have to think about them during the take.

Breathe low and relaxed

Breathe in through the nose or mouth, letting the stomach and the lower ribs move, rather than lifting the shoulders. Keep the shoulders loose and the chest soft. A low, quiet breath is easier to control and sounds more natural on a recording.

Take the breath before the phrase

Breathe a little before the phrase begins, so the sound starts from a settled position. Breathing at the last moment often leads to a rushed start. Practise the timing with the backing, until the breath feels comfortable.

Keep breaths quiet

A loud gasp stands out on a microphone. Breathe more slowly, and keep the mouth open enough that the air does not rush through a small gap. Practise a few breaths in a quiet room, listening on headphones, to hear what the microphone picks up.

Keep some natural breath

Removing every breath from a take makes it sound unnatural. Keep the soft, quiet breaths that fall at phrase ends, and only shorten or lower the ones that distract. The breath editing post covers the same principle for speech.

Limits

Breath control takes practice, and it is different for every singer. If you run out of breath often, a voice teacher can help with technique. Stop and rest if breathing feels painful or tight.

Frequently asked questions

Should I breathe through my nose or mouth?

Either works. Use the method that is quiet and comfortable for you, and mark the breaths in advance.

How do I stop gasping?

Breathe more slowly, relax the shoulders, and practise the breaths quietly with the backing.

Should I remove all breaths when editing?

No. Keep the quiet ones, since they make the performance sound human.

What if I run out of breath in a phrase?

Move the breath earlier, or shorten the phrase by changing the key or the tempo.
