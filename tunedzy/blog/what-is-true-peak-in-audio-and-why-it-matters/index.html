What Is True Peak in Audio and Why It Matters

Updated October 2026 · Loudness and levels

Quick answer

True peak is the highest level the audio reaches when it is turned back into a smooth wave, including the moments between the stored samples. A file can look safe on a sample peak meter and still clip after conversion, so check the true peak of the exported file.

Digital audio stores a series of samples, which are measurements taken at regular points in time. A simple peak meter reads those samples. But the sound that comes out of a speaker is a smooth wave, and that wave can rise higher between the samples. When it does, the output can clip even though the meter never reached the top.

Why sample peaks can mislead

Imagine two samples that are both just below full scale. The wave between them can rise above full scale, and a converter or a lossy encoder can overshoot. The result is a distorted sound that the sample meter did not show. This happens most with loud, dense mixes, and it can also happen after conversion to MP3 or AAC.

What true peak measures

True peak estimates the highest level of the reconstructed wave, including the moments between samples. It is measured in dBTP, which is a dB figure relative to full scale. A true peak below zero means the file should not clip in normal playback, though the exact margin you want depends on the format and the platform.

How to check it

  1. Export the file in the format you will publish.
  2. Open the exported file in a meter that shows true peak.
  3. Check the highest true peak across the whole file, not only the section you were working on.
  4. If it is over your limit, lower the level or limit the peaks, then export again.

Leave headroom

Headroom is the gap between the loudest moment and full scale. A small margin, such as a few decibels for speech, protects the sound when it is converted or played on a device that adds its own processing. The LUFS meter can show the true peak of a finished file alongside its loudness.

Limits

True peak is an estimate. Different tools calculate it in slightly different ways, and some platforms apply their own processing on playback. Use it as a safety check, and listen to the final file on a phone and on headphones before you publish.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my file clip after export when the meter looked fine?

The sample meter misses the peaks between samples. Check the true peak of the exported file.

What does dBTP mean?

It is the true peak level in decibels relative to full scale. A value below zero means no peak reaches full scale.

Do I need to check true peak for speech?

Yes, if the voice is loud or heavily processed. It is a quick check that prevents distortion.

Can a limiter fix true peak?

A limiter can reduce peaks, but use it lightly, or the speech will sound squashed.
