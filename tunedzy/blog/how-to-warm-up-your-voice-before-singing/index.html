How to Warm Up Your Voice Before Singing

Updated October 2026 · Singing at home

Quick answer

Warm up with five to ten minutes of easy breathing, lip trills or humming, gentle sirens across your range, and a few simple scales. Start quietly and gently, and stop if anything hurts or feels strained.

A warm-up gets the breath, the lips, the tongue and the vocal folds ready before you sing properly. It does not need to be long, and it does not need to be dramatic. A short, gentle routine done every time makes singing feel easier and makes recordings sound more even from the first take.

Start with breath

Stand or sit tall with the shoulders relaxed. Breathe in slowly through the nose, letting the stomach and ribs expand, then let the breath out slowly on a soft “sss” for as long as is comfortable. Repeat five times. The aim is steady, relaxed breath, not a deep gasp.

Use lip trills and humming

Lip trills, where the lips buzz as air passes through, warm up the voice without strain. Start low and gentle, and move up and down slowly. Humming on “mmm” does a similar job. Both let you feel the voice without pushing the sound.

Add sirens and simple scales

A siren is a slide from low to high on a single vowel, such as “ooo” or “ee”. Keep it soft and smooth. Then sing a few simple five-note scales on a comfortable vowel, moving up by a semitone each time, and stop well before your range limit.

Warm up at a comfortable volume

Keep the warm-up quieter than your performance volume, especially early in the routine. Loud singing before the voice is ready is the most common way singers strain. If your voice feels rough, slow down and sing more softly.

Know what to avoid

Avoid screaming, forcing the top notes, or singing through a sore throat. Do not warm up while you are straining your voice or after a long shout. If you feel pain, hoarseness that lasts, or a sudden change in your voice, rest and see a doctor or a voice teacher.

Limits

A warm-up helps, but it does not change the basic technique of breath and support. If you struggle with the same problem each time, a voice teacher can give you specific exercises for your voice.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a warm-up take?

Five to ten minutes is enough for most home singing. Longer is not always better.

Should I warm up if I am only recording a short take?

Yes, a short warm-up helps the first take sound more even and reduces strain.

What if my voice feels tired before I start?

Sing softly, rest, and avoid forcing. If it does not improve, leave the session for another day.

Are lip trills good for everyone?

They are gentle and widely used. If anything feels uncomfortable, stop and ask a teacher.
