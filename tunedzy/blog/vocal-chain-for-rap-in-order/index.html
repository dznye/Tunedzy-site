The Vocal Chain for Rap, in Order

Updated October 2026 · Vocal recording fixes

Quick answer

A rap vocal chain works best in this order: clean up noise, high-pass filter, de-ess, EQ, compression, pitch correction if needed, saturation for colour, then reverb and delay on sends. Order matters because each step reacts to what came before: compressing before de-essing makes the sibilance louder.

The order

  1. Clean up. Remove hum, hiss and room noise first, so later steps do not amplify them.
  2. High-pass filter. Around 80 to 100 Hz, to remove thump.
  3. De-ess. Before compression, so it reacts to the real sibilance.
  4. EQ. Remove mud around 200 to 400 Hz and add presence where the words need clarity.
  5. Compression. Even out the loud and quiet words so the lyric stays clear.
  6. Pitch correction, only if the performance needs it, and set to be transparent.
  7. Saturation for warmth, lightly.
  8. Reverb and delay on sends, so the space sits behind the voice and the dry vocal stays clear.

Why the order matters

Put the compressor before the de-esser and it lifts the S sounds along with everything else, so the de-esser has to work harder and sounds worse. Put reverb before compression and the tail gets squashed. Keep the chain in order and each step has a clean input.

Limits

This is a starting point, not a rule. Some voices need less processing, and some styles use more. Change one step at a time, and bypass each to hear what it does.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need every step?

No. Many rap vocals need only cleanup, de-essing, compression and a little reverb.

Where should pitch correction go?

After compression and before the colour and space, so it works on a clean, even signal.

Should reverb be on the vocal track?

Usually on a send, so you can control how much space sits behind the voice.
