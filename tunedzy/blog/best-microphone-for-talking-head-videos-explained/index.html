What to Look for in a Microphone for Talking-Head Videos

Updated October 2026 · Video sound

Quick answer

For talking-head video, look for a microphone that can sit close and steady: a clip-on for the chest, or a small desk or boom microphone for a fixed seat. Check the connection your camera or phone needs, then test the setup before filming, rather than buying by the top of a list.

Many people buy a microphone for a talking-head video and then find the sound is still poor. The reason is usually position and room, not the microphone itself. A modest microphone placed well will always beat an expensive one held far away. Start with the way you will film, then choose the microphone that fits.

Decide how you will sit and move

If you sit in one place, a desk or boom microphone close to the mouth gives a steady sound. If you move, stand or walk, a clip-on microphone on the chest keeps the distance constant. Choose the option that matches how you film, not the one that looks most professional.

Check the connection

Look at what your camera or phone accepts. Some clip-on microphones use a wired plug or a wireless link, and some need an adapter. A microphone that does not connect properly is no use, so check the connector before you buy.

Consider the pattern

For a close speaker, a cardioid or similar pattern helps to reject sound from behind and from the sides. A shotgun microphone can help when it is aimed at the speaker from a short distance, but it needs careful placement and is less forgiving in a busy room.

Test before you film

Record a short test in the actual room, with the camera and the lighting you will use. Play it back on headphones and on a phone. Check for echo, background noise, rustle and the level. A test takes ten minutes and reveals most problems.

Protect the sound

Use a windscreen or pop filter if needed, keep the cable tidy, and make sure clothing does not rub the microphone. Soft furnishings behind you help more than most upgrades. The clap test helps you choose the spot.

Limits

No single microphone suits every room and every speaker. Test the setup you have, and upgrade only when the test shows a clear gap. The best choice is the one that sounds clear in your own room.

Frequently asked questions

Is a clip-on or a desk microphone better?

A clip-on suits moving speakers, and a desk or boom microphone suits a fixed seat.

Do I need a shotgun microphone?

Not usually. A close, steady microphone often sounds better in a home room.

What should I test before filming?

Echo, background noise, rustle and level, on headphones and a phone.

Does the camera microphone work?

It can, but it is usually far from the speaker. A closer microphone almost always sounds better.
