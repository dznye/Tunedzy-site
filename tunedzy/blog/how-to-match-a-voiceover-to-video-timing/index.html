How to Match a Voiceover to Video Timing

Updated October 2026 · Voiceover and narration

Quick answer

Mark the key moments in the video first, then time each voiceover line to land on or before the moment it describes. If a line runs long, shorten the words rather than speeding up the read, and check the sync after every edit.

A voiceover that drifts away from the picture feels careless, even when the words are good. The fix is planning the timing before the final edit, then checking the sync every time the picture changes. Most sync problems come from changing one part of the video without rechecking the rest.

Mark the key moments first

Watch the video once and mark the moments the narration must match: a product appears, a number is shown, a name is spelled on screen, a cut happens. These are the anchors. Everything else can flex around them.

Time each line against its anchor

Place each voiceover line so it starts just before the moment it describes. Leave a short gap before the next anchor, so the viewer has time to see the picture and the words. A line that starts too late makes the narration feel behind the video.

Handle lines that run long

If a line runs past its slot, shorten the words first. Cut filler, combine two sentences, or move part of the line to an earlier moment. Speeding up the read is the last resort, because speed makes narration harder to follow. The script timing guide covers this in more detail.

Keep room for the picture

Viewers need time to read on-screen text and take in an image. A narration that speaks over every second of a slow scene removes that time. Leave short silences where the picture needs them, and let the image lead at a few points.

Check the sync after every change

Each time you trim a shot, move a clip or add a title, check the lines around it. A change in one place often moves the rest by a fraction of a second, which is enough to notice. Play the whole video once after the final changes, not only the parts you edited.

Limits

Some edits need the narration to change, not the picture. If a line will never fit a shot, rewrite the line. A good sync comes from a good script and a patient edit, and both take time.

Frequently asked questions

What should I do if a line is too long for its shot?

Shorten the words first. Speeding up the read is the last resort.

Should narration always match every shot?

No. Leave room for the picture to speak on its own at key moments.

How do I check sync quickly?

Play the whole video once after your final changes, and listen for any line that feels early or late.

Can I fix sync at the end?

Only a little. It is easier to plan the timing before you record the final takes.
