What Microphone to Use for Voice-Over at Home

Updated October 2026 · Voiceover and narration

Quick answer

For most home voice-over, a close-placed dynamic or a small-diaphragm condenser in a quiet room does the job. Choose by how you will connect it and how much the room is working against you, not by the highest price.

Microphone choice matters, but for home voice-over the room and the distance usually matter more. A modest microphone in a soft room, placed close and steadily, will beat an expensive one in a bare room held at arm’s length. Decide what you need from the microphone before you compare models.

Dynamic or condenser

Dynamic microphones are less sensitive to the room and to noise, which suits a home that is not treated. They reject more of the sound behind them, so they are forgiving of a small space. Condenser microphones are more sensitive and capture more detail, which suits a quiet room. They also pick up more background noise and echo, so they need a good space to shine.

USB or XLR

USB microphones plug straight into a computer and are simple to set up. XLR microphones need an audio interface, which adds cost and a step, but they can be upgraded later and give more control over the gain. For a first microphone, USB is usually the simplest route. Choose XLR when you know you want to grow your setup.

Pick the pattern for your room

A cardioid pattern picks up mostly what is in front of the microphone and rejects sound from the back and sides. That is the right choice for most voice-over, because it helps the room behind you stay quiet. Omnidirectional microphones pick up sound from every side and are better left for recording a room, not a voice.

Placement matters more than the model

Place the microphone a hand’s width or two from your mouth, slightly off-axis to reduce breath pops, and keep the distance the same across takes. Put a pop filter in front, and a soft surface behind you. The Noise-Free Voice Recorder can help with the background noise that remains in a home room.

Budget and what to test first

Before you buy, record a minute of your voice in your room with the phone or the computer you already own. Listen on headphones. If the sound is clear and the room is quiet, the next step is a better microphone. If the room is loud or echoey, the next purchase should be treatment, such as soft furnishings, not a microphone.

Limits

No single microphone suits every voice. Try a few if you can, using a return policy, and judge by your own recordings. Reviews and specification sheets help, but they cannot tell you how your voice sounds in your room.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need an expensive microphone for voice-over?

Not usually. A good placement in a quiet, soft room matters more than the price of the microphone.

Is USB good enough?

Yes, for most home voice-over. Choose XLR if you plan to build a larger setup later.

Should I use a dynamic or a condenser?

A dynamic is more forgiving in an untreated room, while a condenser needs a quiet room to sound its best.

What is the first thing to buy?

Often a pop filter and a stand or boom arm. Better placement is cheap and makes a visible difference.
