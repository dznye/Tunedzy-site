How to Get a Future-Style Vocal: Autotune, Reverb and Delay Settings

Updated October 2026

The melodic-trap vocal sound is a short chain: heavy pitch correction on a dry vocal, a touch of saturation, then a big dark reverb and a tempo delay on sends. These are starting points, not a secret recipe. Nobody outside the studio has the exact settings, so finish by ear.

The short version
  • Tune first, to the beat’s key, with a fast retune speed (0–10 ms) for the stepped sound.
  • Add light saturation, then compress; build width with a few doubles.
  • Use a long, dark reverb on a send, high-passed, and a quarter-note delay with low feedback.
  • Duck the effects under the vocal so the words stay clear.

The chain, in order

  1. Clean the vocal. Remove noise, pops and harsh S sounds. Pitch correction reads a dirty vocal badly.
  2. Pitch correction, locked to the key. For the stepped, robotic snap use a retune speed of 0–10 ms. Some records use a slightly slower setting so the pitch bends audibly; try both.
  3. Saturation. A little tape or tube drive adds weight and glue. Keep it subtle or run it on a parallel return.
  4. Compression. Gentle and steady so quiet words stay present.
  5. Doubles and stack. Two or three tuned copies, panned, slightly detuned and timed a few milliseconds apart, make a vocal wide and thick.
  6. Reverb on a send. A long, dark hall or plate, high-passed around 300 Hz so the beat stays tight. The reverb is audible, not hidden.
  7. Delay on a send. A tempo-synced quarter or dotted-eighth, 20–35% feedback, and feed it into the reverb so repeats melt into the tail.
  8. Duck the effects. Lower the reverb and delay while the vocal sings; let them bloom between lines.

What really creates the sound

Three things matter more than any plugin: the key is right, the take is clean, and the effects are balanced against the beat rather than stacked on top of it. Copying settings from a preset pack without checking the key is the most common reason it does not sound right.

Make it yours

Use these numbers as a starting point, then adjust the retune speed, the reverb size and how much of the delay you hear. The goal is a vocal that suits your voice and your beat, not an imitation.

Frequently asked questions

What retune speed gives the Future sound?

A very fast setting, around 0–10 ms, gives the stepped robotic effect. Some records sound slightly slower; try both against your beat.

Do I need Auto-Tune for this sound?

No. The effect comes from fast, key-locked pitch correction, which several tools can do. The key and the clean vocal matter more than the brand.

Why does my version sound thin?

Usually a single vocal with no doubles, no saturation and too little reverb. Add a few detuned doubles, light saturation and a long dark reverb on a send.
