Autotune for Rap and Melodic Vocals: Settings That Work

Updated October 2026

The “autotune sound” is not one setting. It is two choices: which notes the voice is allowed to hit (the key and scale), and how fast it is pulled onto them (retune speed). Get the key right and everything else is taste.

The short version
  • Find your beat’s key and scale first. Wrong key = wrong notes, no matter how good the singing is.
  • Retune speed is the whole sound: slow (>40 ms) is natural, around 10 ms is modern, 0 is the hard robotic snap.
  • Tune a clean, dry vocal before reverb and delay, never after.
  • Rap flow needs timing help too: tune does not fix words that land off the beat.

Step 1: find the key

Pitch correction needs to know which notes are “right”. Ask the producer, look at the project file, or detect it from the beat. Choose the scale (major or minor); if you are unsure, a chromatic setting corrects to the nearest semitone but gives you less of the melodic character.

Step 2: choose the retune speed

  • Natural (slow, around 40–100 ms): invisible correction. Good for singing and for rap you want to stay human.
  • Modern (about 10–30 ms): you hear it on held notes but it still sounds like a person. The sweet spot for many melodic rap and R&B records.
  • Hard (0–5 ms): the snapped, stepped, robotic effect. Every glide becomes a staircase. It is a sound, not a correction.

Step 3: tune a clean vocal, in the right place in the chain

Clean up noise and clicks first, then tune, then compress, then add saturation, reverb and delay. Tuning after reverb makes the tail warble; tuning a noisy take makes the tuner misread the pitch.

Step 4: fix timing, not just pitch

Many rap takes sound “off” because syllables drift from the grid rather than because the notes are wrong. Nudge words onto the beat, especially on repeated patterns and ends of lines, and keep the correction small enough to keep the swing.

Free and online options

Browser-based tuners (Audimee’s free tier, Rys Up’s tool, BandLab’s AutoPitch and similar) are fine for trying the sound on a short clip. Check each one’s limits, watermarks and what they do with your uploaded audio before sending unreleased music. Most online tools get you to a usable melodic sound; they do not match the speed and control of dedicated pitch software.

Common mistakes

  • Setting the wrong key, then blaming the tuner.
  • Using the hard setting on a vocal that was sung out of tune by more than a semitone: the tuner picks the wrong note.
  • Tuning a vocal with bleed from the beat in it.
  • Over-tuning every word, which flattens the emotion. Leave some natural movement in long notes.

Frequently asked questions

What retune speed is best for rap?

It depends on the sound you want. Slower for natural, around 10–30 ms for a modern melodic sound, and 0 for the hard robotic effect.

Do I have to know the key of my beat?

Yes for the best result. If you do not know it, detect it from the instrumental, or use a chromatic setting as a fallback.

Is there a free autotune that works for rap?

Several online tools and free plugins can give a basic melodic effect. Check limits and privacy; they are fine for experiments but may not hold up for a release.
