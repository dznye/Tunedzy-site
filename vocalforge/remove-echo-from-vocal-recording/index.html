How to Remove Echo and Room Reverb From a Vocal Recording

Updated October 2026

Echo and noise are two different problems. Noise sits underneath the voice (hiss, fans, traffic). Echo is the voice itself bouncing back from the walls. That is why a noise-reduction tool will not fix a roomy vocal, and why you can reduce echo but rarely erase it.

The short version
  • Reduce, do not erase: heavy de-reverb makes vocals sound thin, watery or “underwater”.
  • Order: gate or noise-reduce, then de-reverb, then EQ, then compress, then add your own (good) reverb.
  • Recording closer to the mic in a soft room beats any plugin.
  • Add a short, dark reverb on a send afterwards so the vocal does not sound sterile.

First, work out which problem you have

  • Noise: constant sound under and between words: hiss, hum, a fan, an air conditioner. It stays when you stop singing.
  • Room echo (reverb): the tail you hear after each word that dies away. It is the voice, delayed and smeared, so it overlaps the next words.

Noise reduction learns a steady sound and subtracts it. Echo is not steady (it follows your voice), so it needs a different kind of tool: a de-reverb that estimates the late reflections and turns them down.

How to reduce echo, step by step

  1. Start from the original file. Do not process an MP3 export if you still have the WAV.
  2. Remove noise first. Clean steady noise lightly so the de-reverb is not fooled by it.
  3. Apply de-reverb gently. Increase the amount until the tail between words is quieter, then stop. If consonants get soft or the voice sounds hollow, back off.
  4. Re-balance with EQ. De-reverb can leave the vocal slightly thin; a small boost around 150–250 Hz and a cut around 300–500 Hz usually restores body.
  5. Compress after, not before. Compression lifts quiet tails, so compressing a roomy vocal makes the echo louder.
  6. Add a short, dark reverb or delay of your own on a send. A treated vocal with a believable space sounds better than a totally dry, over-processed one.

The thing no plugin fixes

If you recorded in a bathroom, a kitchen or an empty room, the reflections are mixed into the voice at similar loudness to the words. Heavy removal will damage the vocal. Re-record in a closet, under a duvet, or with blankets hung around the mic, even if it feels silly. It takes ten minutes and saves hours.

Free versus paid tools

Free editors have noise reduction and gates that help with hiss and some tails but cannot do true de-reverb. Dedicated tools (iZotope RX, sonible prime:vocal, LALAL.AI’s echo remover and others) do the heavy lifting, and are worth it for important recordings. Whatever you use, compare before and after at the same loudness, and watch for artifacts.

Frequently asked questions

Can you completely remove echo from a vocal?

Not cleanly. You can reduce it a lot on moderate rooms; very roomy recordings can only be improved. Heavy removal causes watery or thin artifacts.

Why does my vocal sound thin after removing reverb?

De-reverb removes some of the body along with the tail. Restore it with a gentle EQ boost in the low-mids and add your own short reverb after.

Is noise reduction the same as removing echo?

No. Noise reduction removes steady background sounds. Echo follows your voice, so it needs de-reverb.
