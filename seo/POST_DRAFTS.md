## Draft 1: a question about a phone recording (r/podcasting, r/voiceacting)

Title: How I got my phone voice recording to sound less like a phone

Body: Most of the difference came from the room, not the app. A small room with clothes or curtains, the fan off, and the phone about 15 to 20 cm from the mouth, a little to the side. After that a noise cleaner took the hiss out. I wrote the steps up here: https://fuidzy.com/tunedzy/clean-up-a-phone-voice-recording (I make the app, Tunedzy; the steps work with any cleaner, and I list the limits).

## Draft 2: a question about hum (r/audioengineering)

Title: Mains hum at 50 or 60 Hz: how to find it and notch it without thinning the voice

Body: Check the spectrum first: hum is a comb of narrow lines at the mains frequency and its multiples. Notch the fundamental, then each harmonic you can see, keeping the notches narrow. Fix the cable path too, because a notch never beats moving the cable. I wrote out the steps: https://fuidzy.com/tunedzy/remove-hum-from-audio . I build Tunedzy, which does this automatically, but the manual steps work in any editor.

## Draft 3: a question about loudness (r/podcasting)

Title: What LUFS target should an episode be?

Body: It depends on where it goes, and platforms change their rules, so check the current guidance. Many creators use around -16 LUFS for stereo podcasts and about -14 LUFS for online video, with the true peak kept below -1 dBTP. A moving-average meter is better than a peak meter for this. The loudness guide explains the numbers: https://fuidzy.com/tunedzy/loudness-for-youtube-podcasts-and-spotify (I make Tunedzy, which sets the target and checks the true peak).
